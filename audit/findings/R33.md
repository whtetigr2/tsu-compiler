# R33 -- the placer is the wrong algorithm for anything that is not already a lattice

**Status:** MEASURED. A structural limitation of `passes/place.py`, not a tuning
problem. The gates, the mediation and the sampling are unaffected.

Reproduce: the measurements below are all cheap and scripted inline; the model is
`out/extropic-verify/codon_spike_full.edges.json`, which ships in the repository.

## Why this was asked

`codon_spike_full` is the largest model this project has published: 3,147 spins,
9,557 edges, degree 12. Every hardware gate passes -- degree 12 of 16, |J| 2.5 of
6, |b| 4.05 of 6, 3,147 of 269,568 nodes -- and it does not place. The obvious
reading is that the search needs a bigger budget. That reading is wrong, and the
question "how much budget" is the wrong question.

## It is not the graph

Three hypotheses, tested in order, all refuted.

**Not an expander.** A subgraph of a 2D lattice has small separators, so an
expander cannot embed. Measured on the largest component: lambda2 of the
normalised Laplacian is 5.0e-05, against 5.5e-03 for a 22x22 grid of comparable
size. It is *less* expanding than a grid.

**Not too dense, and not odd-cycled.** The raw graph has 9,154 triangles and the
Z1 offset lattice has zero -- every offset has odd dx+dy, so the lattice is
bipartite and therefore triangle-free, and a triangle-free host cannot contain a
triangle. But that is what mediation is for, and it works: after
`insert_mediators` the graph is 5,025 spins and 11,435 edges, bipartite, degree
12, **zero triangles**, girth 4 -- the same girth as the lattice.

**Not too big for the plane.** Ball growth, averaged over 120 sources:

    r     |B(v,r)| mediated codon    |B(v,r)| Z1 lattice
    1                        5.2                    16.6
    3                       22.4                   232.2
    6                       44.6                  1034.9

    growth exponent          1.08                    2.27

A subgraph of a 2D lattice cannot exceed exponent 2. The mediated codon graph is
at 1.08 -- nearly one-dimensional, *thinner than the host it has to fit into*.

By every structural test available it should place with room to spare.

## It is the algorithm

`_greedy_descent` starts every node at a uniform random cell and then repeats:
pick a random node, teleport it to a random cell, keep the move if `bad()` did
not increase. `bad()` rescans all 11,435 edges each time. At 40,000 iterations
that is 457 million edge checks, which is the 593 seconds this model takes, and
it accomplishes nothing.

A constructive placer does far better and still fails, and *how* it fails is the
result. Placing breadth-first, each node at a cell legal from every already
placed neighbour, it reaches 13 nodes and dead-ends. The diagnostic on the node
it dies at: degree 6, five neighbours already placed, and across the **entire
infinite lattice** exactly **one** cell is legal from all five. That cell was
already occupied.

That is not bad luck. It is the arithmetic of a sparse lattice:

    already-placed neighbours   cells in the whole lattice legal from all
                            1                                         16
                            2                                          4
                            3                                          2
                            4                                          1
                            5                                          1
                            6                                          1

By four placed neighbours there is typically exactly one legal cell anywhere.
The mediated graph has mean degree 4.55 and max 12, so most nodes are past that
threshold, each gets one chance, and a collision ends the embedding. Restarts do
not help, because every restart meets the same wall in a different place.

## What this means

**Direct placement -- one logical spin to one physical p-bit -- only works when
the logical graph is essentially already a subgraph of the hardware lattice.**
That is why `_try_grid_embed` exists and why it succeeds: grid-shaped graphs ARE
lattice subgraphs, by the identity map. All 18 shipped programs are in that
class, which is why all 18 place.

For anything else, the operation this pass performs is subgraph isomorphism into
a fixed sparse lattice with no slack anywhere, and it fails for reasons that have
nothing to do with whether the hardware could host the model.

The rest of the field does not do this. Minor embedding gives each logical spin a
**chain** of physical p-bits, strongly coupled so they act as one variable. A
chain can be reached from many directions, which dissolves the
one-legal-cell problem entirely: the neighbour attaches to whichever cell of the
chain is convenient. D-Wave has worked this way since Chimera, and Extropic's own
published path uses chains too.

`tsu-compiler` has no chain embedder. Its placement layer is therefore the right
tool for lattice-shaped workloads and the wrong tool for general ones.

## What is NOT affected

Worth stating precisely, because "the placer is wrong" invites a larger
conclusion than the evidence supports.

- **Gates.** Unaffected. Degree, |J|, |b|, node budget and colouring are
  properties of the model, measured before placement, and they are what answers
  "does anything in Z1's published limits refuse this". `codon_spike_full`
  passes all of them and that result stands.
- **Mediation.** Unaffected and exactly verified. It does what it claims --
  makes the graph 2-colourable so block Gibbs has a schedule -- and the
  measurements above confirm it on this model: triangles 9,154 to 0.
- **Sampling.** Unaffected. Placement is not on the sampling path;
  `audit/codon_sampleability.py` samples this model without placing it, and the
  distribution is checked against brute-force enumeration elsewhere.

The claim "will it fit the hardware" rests on the gates and survives. The claim
"and here is where it goes on the die" rests on the placer and does not, for
anything that is not lattice-shaped.

## What would fix it

A minor embedder: chains, with the standard machinery -- chain length budget,
chain-strength setting, and a gate for chain length against the node budget. That
is a known algorithm, not research. It is also the difference between a preflight
tool and a placement tool, and the repository should not describe itself as the
second until it has one.
