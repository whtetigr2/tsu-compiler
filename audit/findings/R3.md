## The three conventions, and where the sign flip actually happens

Three conventions are genuinely in play, confirmed by direct reading, not
inferred:

1. **The IR** (`src/tsu_compiler/ir.py:6-8`): `E(x) = sum over terms of weight *
   term(x)`, `p(x) proportional to exp(-beta*E(x))`, penalty terms carry
   POSITIVE weight (confirmed: `MONOTONE_PENALTY=10.0`,
   `ONE_HOT_PENALTY=10.0`, both positive constants, `encode.py:31,62`).
2. **thrml's own convention** (`thrml/models/ising.py:23-27`, read from the
   installed package, not `src/tsu_compiler`): `E_thrml(s) = -beta*(sum_i b_i*s_i +
   sum_{ij} J_ij*s_i*s_j)`, and `IsingEBM.factors`/`SpinEBMFactor.energy`
   (`thrml/models/discrete_ebm.py:180-183`) implement exactly this: each
   factor's energy is `-sum(weight * spin_product)`, with `weight = beta *
   bias` or `beta * J` respectively (`ising.py:70-75`) -- so `E_thrml` is a
   **second, independent negation**, entirely internal to the `thrml`
   package, not something this project's code performs or controls.
3. **The oracle** (`audit/oracles/exact.py`, written from the physics,
   never importing `src/tsu_compiler`): `E(s) = -sum J_ij*s_i*s_j - sum b_i*s_i`, `s
   in {-1,+1}`, `p(s) proportional to exp(-beta*E(s))`.

**The sign flip between (1) and the `(b, J)` pair used everywhere downstream
happens in exactly one place**: `lower.py:318-320` (sparse path) /
`lower.py:156-157` (dense oracle path, retained as a test oracle only) --
`acc = {k: -v for k, v in acc.items()}`, a single negation, stated in the
module's own docstring ("`sum b s + sum J s s == -E(x)`. The sign flip is
applied ONCE, here.") and confirmed by direct reading: there is no other
`-` applied to an energy-bearing quantity anywhere else in `lower()`. thrml's
own internal negation (item 2 above) is a **separate, external convention**
this project does not touch, and it is not the "second flip" the plan's
own warning is about -- it belongs to a different library, applies
unconditionally regardless of what `(b, J)` it is handed, and (per the
numeric check below) composes correctly with `lower.py`'s one flip to
reproduce `p(x) proportional to exp(-beta*E(x))` to 5.6e-17. A **true**
accidental double-flip inside this project's own code (e.g. a second `-`
inserted somewhere in `lower()` or `energy_of_draw`) is exactly what the
asymmetric-instance test below is built to catch, and it does not find one.

`offset` (`IsingModel.offset`, `lower.py:33`, `:337`) carries the
state-independent constant of `E(x)` (not of `-E(x)` -- `offset = float(-acc.get((), 0.0))`,
the negation of `-E(x)`'s own constant term, i.e. `E(x)`'s own constant term
restated positively). It is added, not subtracted, in both
`energy_of_draw` (`demo/lattice_app.py:1080`, `total = im.offset; total -=
...`) and `search.py`'s `_from_ising` (`:745`, `total = ising.offset; total
+= -...`, algebraically identical). `offset` is a per-state-**constant**
shift, so it must never move a state's *relative* probability, only its
*absolute* energy number -- exactly what a shift-invariant softmax
guarantees, and exactly what is verified numerically below.

## (a) Three-way energy agreement -- the numbers

Instance: 3 binary spins, biases `{x0: 0.7, x1: -0.3, x2: 1.1}`, pairwise
weights `{(x0,x1): 0.9, (x1,x2): -0.5, (x0,x2): 0.2}` (all six coefficients
distinct). Lowered via the real `tsu.passes.lower.lower`:
`edges=((0,1),(0,2),(1,2))`, `weights=[-0.225,-0.05,0.125]`,
`biases=[-0.625,0.05,-0.475]`, `offset=0.9`, `beta=1.0`.

| state | `EnergyModel.energy` (IR) | `demo.lattice_app.energy_of_draw` | `oracles.exact.exact_energy` + `im.offset` |
|---|---|---|---|
| (1,1,1) | 2.100000000000 | 2.100000000000 | 2.100000000000 |
| (1,0,1) | 2.000000000000 | 2.000000000000 | 2.000000000000 |
| (1,1,0) | 1.300000000000 | 1.300000000000 | 1.300000000000 |
| (0,0,1) | 1.100000000000 | 1.100000000000 | 1.100000000000 |
| (1,0,0) | 0.700000000000 | 0.700000000000 | 0.700000000000 |
| (0,1,1) | 0.300000000000 | 0.300000000000 | 0.300000000000 |
| (0,0,0) | 0.000000000000 | -0.000000000000 | 0.000000000000 |
| (0,1,0) | -0.300000000000 | -0.300000000000 | -0.300000000000 |

`max|IR.energy - energy_of_draw| = 4.441e-16`; `max|energy_of_draw -
(oracle.exact_energy + offset)| = 4.441e-16` -- both at float64 rounding
noise, i.e. exact agreement among all three independently-typed
implementations (the IR's own `EnergyModel.energy`, the demo app's
`energy_of_draw`, and the audit oracle's `exact_energy`, which never
imports `src/tsu_compiler`).

A fourth, non-mandatory cross-check against **thrml's own energy route**
(`exact_distribution`, which builds a real `IsingEBM` and calls
`jax.nn.softmax(-ebm.energy(...))`) on the same instance:
`max|p_demo - p_thrml| = 5.551e-17`. All four independently-implemented
routes to "the probability of a draw" -- IR energy, demo energy, oracle
energy, and thrml's own softmax -- agree to machine precision on an
instance with no symmetry to hide a cancelling error behind.

## (b) Sign-flip-of-J falsification test -- had real teeth

Same instance, every pairwise weight negated (`0.9,-0.5,0.2` ->
`-0.9,0.5,-0.2`), re-lowered (`offset` changes too: `0.9 -> 0.6`, correctly,
since the constant term of `E(x)` depends on the coupling signs). Energies
again agree three ways to `4.4e-16`/`2.2e-16`. The two induced Boltzmann
distributions (beta=1):

| state | p(J>0) | p(J<0) |
|---|---|---|
| (0,1,0) | 0.3033079146 | 0.2364425758 |
| (0,0,0) | 0.2246960296 | 0.1751609683 |
| (0,1,1) | 0.1664589129 | 0.0477369328 |
| (1,0,0) | 0.1115807461 | 0.0869823627 |
| (1,1,0) | 0.0612368118 | **0.2887916142** |
| (0,0,1) | 0.0747948109 | 0.0583060213 |
| (1,0,1) | 0.0304093008 | 0.0353643896 |
| (1,1,1) | 0.0275154732 | 0.0712151353 |

**Total variation distance between p(J>0) and p(J<0): 0.276210.** State
(1,1,0) flips from the *second-least*-likely to the *single most*-likely
state under the sign flip -- exactly the qualitative reversal a genuine
coupling-sign flip should produce, and nothing close to 0.0 (which is what
a double-flip-cancels bug, or a test with no teeth, would show). Both
distributions independently agree with `oracles.exact.exact_boltzmann` to
`5.551e-17` in both signs. **This falsification test found no defect, and
it was capable of finding one** -- the asymmetric instance guarantees a
double sign-flip bug could not hide behind a symmetric energy landscape,
and the 0.276 TV distance is direct evidence the test discriminates.

## Where else beta enters

Beta enters the probability exactly where the IR says it does (thrml's
`beta * biases` / `beta * weights` inside `IsingEBM.factors`,
`ising.py:70-75`) -- confirmed numerically above. It enters a **second,
physically distinct** place: `route.py:195`'s mediator-gadget formula `A =
arccosh(exp(2*beta*|J|))/(2*beta)`, which determines the *coupling value*
baked into a mediator spin at compile time, not a second rescaling of the
same energy. This is not a duplicate or redundant application of beta (it
is folded into `J` itself before thrml ever multiplies by beta again), and
it is exactly why a mediated model cannot be resampled at a different beta
without reproducing the wrong physical couplings -- `route.
assert_beta_consistent` refuses this (confirmed to fire correctly in
R7.md's own mediator tests). Worth stating explicitly because "where does
beta enter" has two correct answers in this codebase, not one, and
conflating them would be a plausible source of confusion for a future
reader.

## Findings

### Cross-reference (full detail in R14.md, not re-litigated here)

**See R14.md's NaN/Inf-ingress findings**: `lower()` (`lower.py:44-342`)
performs no validation on a term's `weight` or on `EnergyModel.beta` --
a `NaN`/`inf` value in either propagates silently into a "successfully
lowered" `IsingModel` with no error and no `unavailable:` label. This sits
in the same file this section audited and is a genuine defect, but its
nature (missing input validation, not a wrong sign/energy formula) makes
it R14's finding, not R3's -- recorded here only as a pointer so a reader
of this file is not left wondering whether R3 checked NaN ingress (it did
not; R14 did, exhaustively).

### What was tried and did not turn up a finding

- Three independently-typed computations of `E(x)` (IR, demo app, audit
  oracle) on an asymmetric instance with no relabelling/sign symmetry:
  agree to float64 rounding noise (`4.441e-16`). No finding.
- A fourth cross-check against thrml's own `IsingEBM.energy`/softmax route
  (not required by the plan, added because "the Boltzmann factor" and
  "where beta enters" are explicitly this section's questions): agrees to
  `5.551e-17`. No finding.
- Sign-flip-of-J on the same asymmetric instance: produces a real,
  qualitative reordering of the distribution (TV=0.276, a specific state's
  rank inverting), confirmed against the oracle in both signs to
  `5.551e-17`. The test has teeth; no double-flip-cancels defect found.
- Checked whether `offset` could be silently dropped or double-counted
  anywhere between `lower()`, `insert_mediators()` (which adds an
  `offset_correction` per mediated edge, `route.py:203-206,218`), and
  `energy_of_draw`/`_from_ising`: R7.md's own mediator-marginal test
  (which depends on `offset` cancelling correctly under normalisation)
  independently confirms this to `8.327e-17` across four (J, beta)
  configurations. No finding.
- Checked whether `lower()`'s single sign flip and thrml's own internal
  negation could compose into a net double-flip: they compose to `E_thrml
  = beta*E(x)` exactly, verified numerically (`max|p_demo - p_thrml| =
  5.551e-17`), not `E_thrml = -beta*E(x)`. No finding.
- Checked whether penalty terms actually carry positive weight as the IR's
  own docstring requires: `MONOTONE_PENALTY=10.0`, `ONE_HOT_PENALTY=10.0`
  (`encode.py:31,62`), both positive, confirmed by direct reading. Not
  independently type-enforced anywhere (a spec author could write a
  negative-weight "penalty" and nothing would catch the misnomer), but
  this is a naming-discipline observation, not a computed-wrong-answer
  defect -- not filed as a finding.

**Verdict: PASS.** Every mandatory test the plan specifies for R3 was run,
against an instance chosen specifically to be hard to fool, and every one
of them confirms the stated conventions to machine precision. No Critical,
Important, or Minor finding is filed against energy semantics itself.
