# R22 -- the coupling gate is beta-independent, and I read a hardware conclusion out of it that was not there

**Status:** FOUND, DOCUMENTED, PINNED. No shipped result is affected. The defect
was in an inference I made and sent to a collaborator, and it was caught before
it reached anyone outside the project -- but only just.

**Method:** Grok ran Check 2 of `audit/GROK_VERIFICATION_DISPATCH.md`, a beta
ladder on `codon_spike_full`, and found a hardness onset at beta = 1.5. I tried
to translate that ladder into Z1's coupling units to say whether the hard regime
was representable on the hardware. It is that translation that was wrong.

## What I claimed

Because `p ~ exp(-beta * E)`, beta and J enter the distribution only through
their product. So I reasoned that scaling beta by 1.5 on a model with
`|J|max = 2.5` is equivalent to `J_eff = 3.75` at beta = 1, and compared that to
Z1's published cap of 6.0. From that I told Grok, in writing:

> **THE GOOD NEWS, and it is the real finding:** the hardness onset sits at
> J_eff = 3.75, **inside** Z1's addressable coupling range.

and

> Please mark beta >= 3 explicitly **NOT REPRESENTABLE ON Z1**.

Grok agreed and began binding an Extropic-facing artifact to both.

## Why both were wrong

**Two errors, one arithmetic and one conceptual.**

The arithmetic one: I quoted a fixed post-mediation ceiling of 5.6534 in J_eff
units. That is the beta = 1 value only. In J_eff units the ceiling is
`ln(cosh(2*beta*cap))/2`, which *grows* with beta -- 5.65 at beta=1, 8.65 at
beta=1.5, 11.65 at beta=2. There is no fixed ceiling across the ladder.

The conceptual one matters more. I reasoned about what the gate *should* do
instead of checking what it *does*. Measured:

```
 beta   reported |J|max   gate limit   passes?
  1.0        2.5000          6.0        True
  1.5        2.5000          6.0        True
  2.0        2.5000          6.0        True
  6.0        2.5000          6.0        True
100.0        2.5000          6.0        True
```

`gates.py` reads `peak_J = np.abs(ising.weights).max()` -- the raw weight, no
beta factor -- and `analyse.py` never references beta. **The coupling gate is
beta-independent.** A model at beta = 100 with `|J| = 2.5` passes the same cap
as one at beta = 1, though it specifies a dimensionless coupling of 250.

So the ladder does not test the cap at all. Every rung passes identically, and
no statement about representability -- in either direction -- follows from it.

## The underlying open question, which is genuinely unresolved

Whether the gate is right depends on something no source available to this
project settles:

> Does Extropic's `Jmax <= 6.0` bound the dimensionless product `beta*J`, or a
> programmable register value at a fixed hardware operating temperature?

Thermalizers states `|J| <= Jmax` without resolving it. The two readings
**coincide exactly at beta = 1** and diverge everywhere else. This is an
assumption-level open question of the same kind as `max_abs_bias`, and the
project's rule is to label those rather than guess -- which is precisely what I
failed to do.

**It cannot be resolved by inference. It needs a source.**

## Blast radius: none shipped

Every model this project has compiled or published is **beta = 1.0** --
verified across all eleven edge files in `out/extropic-verify/`. At beta = 1 the
two readings agree exactly, so no published verdict depends on the answer. The
limit bites only when someone scales beta, which is exactly what Check 2 did.

## What was actually salvageable from Check 2

The measurement stands; only my hardware bridge was fiction:

> Under a construction-scaled beta ladder, CPU block-Gibbs sampling of
> `codon_spike_full` certifies every spin at beta = 0.5 and beta = 1.0
> (Extropic's published point) and exhausts a 256,000-draw budget with spins
> uncertified from beta = 1.5 upward. This measures the difficulty of OUR
> sampling procedure on a model WE constructed by scaling beta. It makes no
> statement about Z1, about representability, or about hardware performance.

Also worth keeping separate: "the harness exhausted its budget" is a fact about
our run; "the model is intractable" is a claim about the model. Only the frozen
rows (spins with zero variance) are unambiguous, and those sit at beta >= 3.

## The honest reckoning

This is the same failure this audit has now caught four times, except this time
I committed it rather than found it. R20 was a correct measurement wrapped in an
unsupported sentence. R21 was an assertion whose message overstated it. R22 is a
**correct measurement wrapped in a hardware conclusion the tooling cannot
support** -- and I produced it while auditing other people's claims for exactly
that property.

The specific trap is worth naming: **the error was the flattering direction.**
"The hard regime is inside Z1's range" is the answer that makes the project look
good, and it is the one I reached without checking the gate. The arithmetic
error was trivial and would have been caught by anyone. The conceptual error was
load-bearing and was heading into an Extropic-facing artifact with a
collaborator's agreement behind it.

That is the argument for the dispatch's own rule -- *every check must name in
advance what result would be bad news* -- applying to conclusions and not only
to experiments.

## What was done

- Retracted both claims to Grok in Model Chat before the artifact was written,
  explicitly including the flattering one.
- `src/tsu_compiler/gates.py` -- the scope of the coupling gate is documented at
  the gate itself: what it bounds, what it does not, why the question is open,
  and that beta-scaled coupling verdicts must not be read as representability.
- `tests/test_coupling_gate_is_beta_independent.py` (8 tests) pins the property
  so it cannot drift silently, including that a beta-scaled and a J-scaled model
  specifying the SAME distribution receive OPPOSITE verdicts, and a test that
  fires the moment any shipped workload stops being beta = 1.0 -- the point at
  which this stops being harmless.

The tests deliberately do **not** assert the gate is correct. They assert it is
what it is. If a real source ever settles the Jmax semantics, the change should
be a deliberate edit to a documented property, not a silent drift.
