# R23 -- the visibility demo's couplings were inert, and I said they were the point

**Status:** FOUND, and it invalidates a claim that was live on a public page for
several hours. The underlying idea survives; the configuration it was
demonstrated in did not.

**Method:** porting the visibility model to THRML forced me to write the energy
out explicitly as couplings and biases rather than as a Gibbs update rule. The
algebra then said something my prose did not.

## What was claimed

`audit/visibility_as_inference.py`, the `visibility.html` demo, and the
`level.html` demo all said the same thing:

> Occlusion is not computed by taking the nearest hit; it is a constraint the
> energy will not let you break. A stopped cell forces everything behind it to
> have stopped.

And the commit message for the first of those asserted the mechanism was an
ASYMMETRIC monotonicity coupling, with a symmetric one explicitly called out as
the wrong thing that had been tried and failed.

## Both halves are false

**The asymmetry does nothing.** The update I described as asymmetric was

    +W_MONO * [v_{k+1} = 1]   -   W_MONO * [v_{k-1} = 0]

Substituting `v = (s+1)/2`, the constants cancel exactly and this is

    (W_MONO/2) * (s_{k-1} + s_{k+1})

a plain symmetric ferromagnetic coupling at half strength. Measured on the same
level: asymmetric clauses give 100.0%, symmetric at the same strength gives
100.0%, and without the clamp both give 40.6%. **The clamp was the entire fix,**
and I attributed it to the wrong term.

**The couplings were doing nothing at all.** Turning each term off in turn:

    chain  free-space  coherence  clamp   agreement  early stops
        6           3        0.6   True      100.0%            0
        0           3        0.6   True      100.0%            0   <- no chain
        6           0        0.6   True        0.0%           64
        6           3        0.0   True      100.0%            0   <- no coherence
        6           3        0.6  False       40.6%            0

Only the clamp and the free-space bias matter. With both present and neither
coupling, every cell is independent: a free cell takes 1 with probability
sigmoid(2*beta*W_OCC) = 0.999994, a wall is clamped to 0, and "first 0" is
simply "first wall".

**So the decode was performing the occlusion, not the energy.** `first index
where v == 0` is a linear scan for the nearest wall. That is a raycast written
in a different notation, with a lattice running beside it contributing nothing.

Worse, the clamp made the lattice incapable of helping even in principle. A
spurious wall is clamped off, and no coupling can outvote a clamp -- which is
why adding sensor noise produced a gain of **exactly +0.0%** at every level
tested.

## The idea survives, in a different configuration

Make the wall a strong BIAS instead of an absolute clamp and the couplings
acquire something to do -- they can outvote a wall the input got wrong:

    noise   no couplings   coherence only   full lattice     gain
    0.000          96.9%           100.0%          98.4%    +1.6%
    0.010          75.0%            76.6%          89.1%   +14.1%
    0.020          54.7%            54.7%          85.9%   +31.2%
    0.040          32.8%            32.8%          79.7%   +46.9%
    0.080          12.5%            12.5%          67.2%   +54.7%

At 8% corrupted occupancy the lattice takes 12.5% to 67.2%. That is the energy
doing work no scan can do: recovering a coherent surface from an input that is
individually unreliable but jointly constrained.

**And it tells you exactly when sampling is worth anything.** With perfect
occupancy the couplings buy +1.6%, which is a rounding error -- when the input is
certain, a scan is the right tool and a lattice is decoration. The lattice earns
its keep precisely where the input does not already contain the answer.

That is a better demonstration than the one it replaces, and it is the honest
one. The original showed a sampler reproducing an answer that was already
present in its input, and called the reproduction inference.

## Why this got past everything

The controls were real and all of them passed. Agreement reached 100%, free-space
stops reached zero, the cold control sat at 0%, the lattice was bipartite, THRML
reproduced it. **Every one of those is consistent with the couplings being
inert,** because none of them asked whether the couplings were load-bearing.

The check that would have caught it is the one I never wrote: turn a term off
and see whether the answer changes. An ablation is not a control against noise,
it is a control against irrelevance, and this project did not have one.

Same shape as R20 once again -- a correct measurement wrapped in a sentence the
evidence does not support. The measurements here were all fine. "The energy will
not let you break occlusion" was not.

## What has to change

- `audit/visibility_as_inference.py` -- soft walls, plus an ablation table, plus
  a noise sweep. The claim it supports is about denoising, not about occlusion.
- `audit/visibility_on_thrml.py` -- same, and the THRML result stands either way
  since it reproduces whatever energy it is given.
- `visibility.html` and `level.html` -- both say occlusion is enforced by the
  energy. Both are live. Both need correcting, not softening.

## Credit

Paul said "we MUST use THRML." Writing the model out as real couplings for
someone else's sampler is what exposed this; it would have stayed hidden behind
a hand-written update rule indefinitely.

## Resolution

All four items are done, and the corrected claim is measured separately on each
artefact rather than quoted across them -- the models differ in size, so one
page's numbers are not another's.

`audit/visibility_as_inference.py` -- soft walls, ablation table, noise sweep.
Clean input +4.7%, 8% noise 12.5% -> 70.3%.

`audit/visibility_on_thrml.py` -- rewritten. The clamp is gone, the constants
are IMPORTED from the reference module so the two cannot drift, and the file now
runs two checks instead of one: thrml against numpy on the same energy, and an
ablation through thrml itself.

           input   couplings     thrml     numpy     gap
           clean         off     93.5%     95.8%    2.3%
           clean          on     99.7%    100.0%    0.3%
        8% noise         off      9.4%     10.4%    1.0%
        8% noise          on     63.8%     60.4%    3.4%

    coupling gain, clean input:     +6.2%
    coupling gain, 8% corrupted:   +54.4%

That is the finding closed properly: Extropic's library, sampling the same
couplings, shows the gain is in the energy and not in a loop I wrote.

`visibility.html` -- energy corrected, noise slider and couplings toggle added,
prose replaced. Measured on the page itself, 96 columns, 7 runs x 150 sweeps:
clean 100.0% off / 99.9% on, 8% noise 11.9% -> 83.8%. The clean-input gain is
NEGATIVE (-0.1%) and says so on the page.

`level.html` -- same correction, plus the controls. Measured on the level, 200
columns, 7 runs: clean 100% either way (gain exactly 0.0 in all seven runs),
8% noise 3.9% -> 83.5%.

Both pages expose `ablate()` on the console, which re-settles from random for
every cell of the table and prints it. A claim about an ablation ought to be
re-runnable by whoever is reading it.

Two further defects fell out of the correction, neither caught by any control:

- `visibility_as_inference.build_ising` wrote weights as -W_MONO and -W_COH.
  Positive is ferromagnetic in this IR, so those were ANTIferromagnetic, and the
  chain was at full strength where `sample()` applies half to each neighbour.
  Nothing measured was wrong, because `build_ising` only ever fed the preflight
  -- but `visibility_on_thrml.py` samples it now, so it had to be the same
  energy. Fourth appearance of the sign trap.
- The stale "MONOTONICITY IS ASYMMETRIC, and that is the whole point" comment
  was still sitting directly above the corrected one, so the file asserted both
  the claim and its retraction. Removed.

And one process note. The first attempt at the `level.html` controls put the
event handlers OUTSIDE the page's IIFE, where the variables they assign to do
not exist. The page loaded with zero console errors, because nothing had clicked
yet. It was caught by clicking the button rather than by reading the diff --
the same lesson as the mojibake: look at the thing, and operate it.
