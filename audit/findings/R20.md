# R20 -- the codon sampleability harness folded a non-symmetric order parameter

**Status:** FOUND and FIXED. The defect was in the DIAGNOSTIC, not the sampler.
The conclusion it supported did not change; the evidence under it is now much
stronger. Nothing in `src/` was wrong.

**Method:** the claims-vs-evidence audit the project owner asked for -- "is the
claim really what our evidence says?" -- applied to `audit/codon_sampleability.py`,
which produces this project's headline sampling result.

## What the harness did

It judged whether each of Extropic's published codon models could be sampled
from a single scalar:

```python
mag = (2 * draws.astype(np.int8) - 1).mean(axis=2)
est = effective_sample_size(np.abs(mag))
rh  = float(r_hat(np.abs(mag)))
```

Three things are wrong with those three lines, in increasing order of severity.

### 1. `abs()` has no justification on these models

Folding the magnetisation by `abs()` is standard for a **Z2-symmetric** Ising
model: with no external field the energy is invariant under flipping every spin,
the sign of `m` is arbitrary, and `+m` and `-m` are the same physics. Folding
recovers a usable order parameter across that symmetry.

These models have no such symmetry. Measured directly:

| workload | spins | spins with a nonzero bias | max \|b\| |
|---|---|---|---|
| codon_tiny_10aa | 31 | **31** | 2.991 |
| codon_default_prefix | 266 | **266** | 3.500 |
| codon_spike_200aa | 481 | **481** | 3.500 |
| codon_spike_full | 3,147 | **3,147** | 4.050 |

Every spin carries a field. Flipping all spins is not a symmetry of the energy,
so `+m` and `-m` are genuinely different states -- and `abs()` maps them onto
the same number. Two chains sitting in real, distinct modes at `m = +0.3` and
`m = -0.3` would produce an R-hat of 1.00. The diagnostic would report health it
had not earned. That is the exact failure this project exists to refuse.

For contrast, `src/tsu_compiler/preflight/sweep.py:249` also uses `abs(m)` --
in the finite-size susceptibility of a uniform square lattice in zero field.
That model **is** Z2-symmetric and the fold there is correct. The error was not
"we used abs()"; it was using it where the symmetry does not hold.

### 2. It measured mediator spins as if they were part of the model

`mean(axis=2)` averaged over all **physical** spins, including the mediators
this compiler inserted to fix bipartite parity. Those are auxiliary variables
the model never asked for; averaging them into the order parameter dilutes the
statistic with 1,878 spins of our own creation on the full spike.

### 3. One scalar cannot certify thousands of variables

This is the deepest of the three. A chain can mix perfectly in the mean while an
individual spin never flips once. Reporting R-hat and ESS for a single summary,
when the standard multi-parameter practice is max R-hat and min ESS across
parameters, meant the verdict rested on a statistic that structurally cannot see
the failure it was supposed to rule out.

## What the correct measurement shows

Same draws, same seed, 16 chains x 4,000 samples, order parameter restricted to
logical spins:

| workload | tau (\|m\|) | tau (signed) | ESS (\|m\|) | ESS (signed) | **ESS inflation from folding** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| codon_default_prefix | 4.11 | 5.79 | 15,557 | 11,051 | **+41%** |
| codon_spike_200aa | 4.34 | 5.30 | 14,755 | 12,084 | **+22%** |
| codon_spike_full | 4.98 | 5.28 | 12,842 | 12,125 | **+5.9%** |

Folding understated tau and inflated ESS on every workload, in the same
direction each time. The headline ESS was optimistic.

And the per-spin pass, which is the measurement that should have been there
from the start:

| workload | logical spins | **max R-hat** | median R-hat | **min ESS** | median ESS | frozen |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| codon_tiny_10aa | 31 | 1.0013 | 1.0006 | 7,580 | 11,586 | **0** |
| codon_default_prefix | 266 | 1.0030 | 1.0003 | 5,280 | 18,808 | **0** |
| codon_spike_200aa | 481 | 1.0025 | 1.0002 | 5,224 | 20,864 | **0** |
| codon_spike_full | **3,147** | **1.0032** | 1.0002 | **5,045** | 22,919 | **0** |

## The honest reading

**The claim survives, and the audit made it stronger rather than weaker.** Every
logical spin in Extropic's full 3,147-spin spike model mixes: the worst spin of
3,147 has R-hat 1.0032 and ESS 5,045, and not one spin is frozen. That is a
substantially better-evidenced statement than "the scalar ESS was 6,837", which
was one number that could not have detected a stuck spin at all.

What must be corrected downward is the **ESS figure itself**, which the fold
inflated. Any document quoting `ESS 6837` is quoting a folded statistic.

## What was NOT wrong

- The sampler. `sample_chains` is thrml's own block-Gibbs entry point, and
  `tests/test_thrml_matches_exact.py` checks it against a brute-force oracle
  that does not import this package.
- The ESS estimator. `tests/test_ess.py` cross-checks it against `statsmodels`,
  `arviz`, an analytic AR(1) table, and Geyer's initial-positive-sequence.
- The R-hat implementation. `tests/test_preflight_diagnostics.py` pins the BDA3
  closed form.
- `insert_mediators`. The marginal-preservation property is verified exactly
  (R7), and mediator ordering is asserted in the harness rather than assumed.

Every component was individually correct and independently validated. The defect
was in how they were **composed into a claim** -- which is the same shape as
every other defect this audit has found: a correct measurement wrapped in a
sentence the data does not support.

## Files changed

- `audit/codon_sampleability.py` -- signed order parameter over logical spins

## Addendum: the first fix was not enough

Switching the order parameter from `|m|` to signed `m` is necessary but **not
sufficient**, and assuming otherwise would have left the same class of hole in
place. `audit/diagnostic_control.py` was written to answer a question this
project had never asked of itself:

> Every workload we have ever measured came back "sampleable". Has the verdict
> function ever been shown capable of returning FAIL?

It had not. A verdict that has only ever passed is not evidence; quoting its
passes is circular. So the control builds a model whose answer is known in
advance -- a 16x16 zero-field Ising lattice, where Onsager gives the exact
critical point Kc = 0.440687 -- and runs the chains deep into the ordered phase,
where sixteen independently started chains provably cannot each have visited
both ground states.

| coupling | beta*J | regime | R-hat on \|m\| | R-hat on signed m | max R-hat per spin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ferromagnetic | 0.20 | disordered | 1.0001 | 1.0001 | 1.0002 |
| ferromagnetic | 0.44 | ~critical | 1.0006 | 1.0390 | 1.0225 |
| ferromagnetic | 0.60 | ordered | **1.0000** | 47.58 | 4.41 |
| ferromagnetic | 0.80 | ordered | **1.0000** | **160.90** | 12.53 |
| antiferromagnetic | 0.20 | disordered | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | 1.0001 |
| antiferromagnetic | 0.44 | ~critical | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | 1.0296 |
| antiferromagnetic | 0.60 | ordered | **0.9999** | **1.0001** | 4.58 |
| antiferromagnetic | 0.80 | ordered | **1.0000** | **1.0000** | 11.39 |

The coupling sign is confirmed against `audit/oracles/exact.py`, which does not
import this package: IR weight -1.0 gives mean neighbour correlation -0.954 and
`<|m|>` = 0.016, i.e. antiferromagnetic. The sign was NOT inferred from our own
IR convention -- this project has shipped a wrong-signed coupling before.

**The ferromagnetic rows are the R20 defect, made visible.** Folding turns an
R-hat of 160.90 into 1.0000. Chains that never mixed are certified healthy.

**The antiferromagnetic rows are worse and were not anticipated.** On a
bipartite lattice the two antiferromagnetic ground states are the two
checkerboards, and *both have net magnetisation zero*. So the folded scalar is
blind and **the signed scalar is blind too** -- every chain reports m ~ 0 while
frozen in a different configuration from its neighbours. Only the per-spin
diagnostic sees it, at max R-hat 11.39.

The disordered rows at beta*J = 0.20 stay quiet on all three measures, so this
is not an alarm that always rings -- the control has specificity as well as
sensitivity.

### The corrected lesson

Not "do not fold a non-symmetric order parameter." That was the surface of it.
The real lesson is that **a scalar summary of any kind can report perfect health
while the chains are catastrophically stuck**, because a mean over thousands of
spins has room to hide any structure that averages out. The verdict must rest on
the individual spins.

### A second defect, introduced by the first fix and caught the same way

The corrected harness reported `min ESS 5,001` alongside the line
`335 spin(s) returned no ESS (below the reliability floor)`. But `min_ess` is the
minimum over spins that *cleared* the floor -- a spin that failed to clear it
does not appear in the minimum at all. Reporting a healthy minimum while
silently excluding the failures is the same defect this finding is about,
reintroduced one layer down.

Fixed: the escalation loop now doubles the draw count until **every** spin is
certified -- `n_ess_unavailable == 0`, `n_frozen == 0`, max R-hat within
threshold -- or the stated budget is exhausted, and the verdict requires all of
them.

### Standing guard

`tests/test_diagnostic_can_report_failure.py` (4 tests) runs the control in fast
form every suite: the per-spin diagnostic must fire in the ordered phase at both
signs, must stay quiet in the disordered phase, and the two blindness results
are pinned so that if either scalar ever stops being blind there, the claim in
this finding has to be rewritten rather than silently drifting.
