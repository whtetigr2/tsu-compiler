## (1) + (2) Domain-wall encode/decode round trip and `is_codeword`, over the FULL codeword space

For k = 2..6 (chain lengths 1..5, i.e. 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 bit patterns
respectively -- **every** pattern, not a sample), each pattern was checked
against a hand-written, independent definition of "codeword" (`all(bits[i]
>= bits[i+1] for i in range(len(bits)-1))` -- monotone non-increasing, the
textbook Chancellor 2019 domain-wall definition), never a restatement of
`encode.py`'s own `is_codeword` logic:

| k | chain length | patterns (full space) | codewords found | expected | `is_codeword` misclassifications | decode round-trip failures | `encode_assignment` round-trip failures |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 3 | 2 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 4 | 3 | 8 | 4 | 4 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 5 | 4 | 16 | 5 | 5 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 6 | 5 | 32 | 6 | 6 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

**`is_codeword` accepts exactly the codewords and rejects exactly the
non-codewords, over the complete bit-pattern space, for every k tested.**
`Encoded.decode` correctly recovers `v` (number of leading 1s) for every one
of the `k` legal codewords at every k, and `encode_assignment(v) -> decode
-> v` round-trips exactly for every `v in 0..k-1`.

## (3) Mediator insertion preserves the marginal -- verified against the oracle, not the existing test suite

A frustrated 3-spin triangle (`edges=(0,1),(1,2),(0,2)`, all three couplings
equal), the canonical case that forces mediation (an odd cycle is never
bipartite), was built directly as an `IsingModel` (bypassing `place`/`route`/
`compile_spec` entirely -- only `analyse()` and `insert_mediators()`, both
fast, pure passes), mediated via the real `tsu.passes.route.insert_mediators`,
and its exact distribution -- both **before** and **after** mediation --
computed via `audit.oracles.exact.exact_boltzmann` (independent of `src/tsu_compiler`).
The mediator spin was summed out of the post-mediation distribution and
compared, state by state, against the oracle's own pre-mediation
distribution:

| configuration | beta | mediators inserted | max\|P(unmediated) - P(mediated, marginalised)\| |
|---|---|---|---|
| J=-1.25 (route.py's own worked example) | 4.0 | 1 | 8.327e-17 |
| J=-1.25 | 1.0 | 1 | 2.776e-17 |
| J=+0.8 (ferromagnetic) | 2.0 | 1 | 2.168e-19 |
| J=+2.4 (large coupling) | 0.5 | 1 | 8.674e-19 |

Every configuration mediates exactly 1 edge (matching the module's own
documented connectivity-residual floor `|E| - maxcut = 3 - 2 = 1` for a
triangle) and produces a bipartite graph (`bipartite_after=True` in every
case). **The mediator's marginal distribution over the original three spins
is indistinguishable from the un-mediated exact distribution to within
`8.3e-17`, across four different (sign, magnitude, beta) configurations --
independently verified against the oracle, not assumed from the existing
test suite or the module's own docstring's hand-derivation claim.**

A second instance closes the single-mediator gap the triangle alone cannot
exercise: **K4** (4-spin complete graph, 6 edges), which needs **2**
mediated edges (connectivity residual `6 - 4 = 2`), tested at two (J, beta)
configurations. `offset_correction` accumulates additively across both
mediated edges (`route.py:206,218`); marginalising out **both** mediator
spins from the oracle's exact distribution of the 6-spin mediated model
reproduces the oracle's exact distribution of the original 4-spin model to
`max|P(unmediated) - P(mediated, 2 mediators marginalised)| = 1.110e-16`
(J=-0.9, beta=1.5) and `1.301e-18` (J=+1.1, beta=1.0). **Multi-mediator
accumulation of `offset_correction` and the marginalisation property both
hold**, not just the single-mediator case.

`assert_beta_consistent` was also exercised on the same mediated models
(directly relevant since mediator couplings are beta-dependent): correctly
raises `BetaMismatchError` when queried at a different beta than the model
was mediated at, and correctly allows sampling at the same beta, in all four
configurations.

## Mediators vs. Thermalizers' chain embedding -- what each preserves, precisely

R2.md (Wave 1) already established these are related but distinct
techniques; this section states precisely what each preserves, grounded in
the numeric verification above rather than restated from the docstring
alone:

- **LATTICE's mediator insertion** (`route.py:150-232`) subdivides a single
  **edge** the target topology cannot place directly (a within-side edge
  after a 2-partition) through one hidden spin. What it provably preserves,
  confirmed numerically above: **the exact joint marginal distribution over
  the original logical spins**, for any sign/magnitude of the mediated
  coupling and any beta, once the mediator is summed out. The unit of
  subdivision is an edge; the technique fixes a *bipartite-parity*
  conflict (the target's own topology constraint), not a degree/fan-out
  limit.
- **Thermalizers' chain embedding** (per R2.md's citation of the
  `thermalizers` paper) subdivides a single overcommitted logical **spin**
  into a chain of many physical p-bits (worked example: 336 logical spins
  -> 1774 physical p-bits on chains up to 18 long). This is the minor-
  embedding technique familiar from quantum annealers: its job is to
  amplify effective degree/connectivity for a spin that needs to touch more
  neighbours than the hardware's own degree allows, and its correctness
  property is that the chain of physical p-bits, coupled ferromagnetically
  to each other, acts as one effective logical spin (via a strong
  intra-chain coupling forcing agreement, not via marginalisation of a
  gadget). This audit did not re-derive Thermalizers' own chain-embedding
  correctness proof (no reference implementation of it exists in this
  codebase or repo to test against -- `torx`/`thermalizers` code present
  here never implements chain embedding, confirmed by R2.md's own
  correspondence table) and does not claim to have verified it; the
  distinction stated here is definitional (unit of subdivision: edge vs.
  node; preserved quantity: exact marginal via a gadget vs. effective
  single-spin behaviour via strong intra-chain coupling), not a numeric
  comparison of the two.

**These are not interchangeable and must never be described as one
project "implementing" the other's technique** -- R2.md already established
this at the level of documentation/vocabulary; this section adds the
numeric confirmation that LATTICE's own technique does what it claims.

## Findings

No Critical or Important findings. The representation layer -- domain-wall
codeword encode/decode/`is_codeword` over the complete bit-pattern space,
and mediator insertion's marginal-preservation property, verified
independently against the oracle rather than trusted from the existing test
suite -- holds exactly, to the limits of float64 precision, across every
configuration tested.

### Minor

**M-3: the mediator-vs-chain-embedding distinction (this section's own
"what each preserves" analysis) exists only in `route.py`'s module
docstring, R2.md, and this file -- not in any user-facing text.** This
restates R2.md's own T-2 finding (not a new one) with R7's added numeric
grounding; not re-filed as a separate item, cited here for completeness
since R7's own brief explicitly asked for this contrast.

### What was tried and did not turn up a finding

- Checked one-hot encoding's own `is_codeword`/`decode` (the second
  encoding this compiler supports, `_encode_one_hot`/`Encoded.decode`'s
  one-hot branch, `encode.py:101-103,147-155,174-176`) is not the R7 brief's
  named focus ("domain-wall encode/decode round-trip"), but was read in
  full: `is_codeword` requires exactly one spin set to 1 per chain,
  `decode` raises (rather than guessing an index) on a non-codeword,
  exactly matching the codeword-gating discipline domain-wall's own
  `is_codeword`/`decode` pair uses. Not independently full-space-tested
  numerically (out of this section's explicit scope), so no verdict is
  claimed on one-hot beyond this reading -- recorded as an explicit
  boundary of this section's coverage, not a pass/fail claim.
- Checked whether `insert_mediators`'s `bipartite_after` measurement
  (`route.py:225-228`, a real `nx.is_bipartite` call on the mediated graph,
  not an assumption) could be wrong for a graph this module itself
  constructs: cross-checked structurally against the exact-distribution
  test above (a non-bipartite mediated model would still have a
  well-defined marginal via the oracle regardless, so this check is
  orthogonal to marginal-preservation) -- `bipartite_after=True` in all
  four configurations, consistent with a triangle needing exactly one
  mediated edge to become bipartite. No finding (R8's territory for a
  fuller bipartiteness audit; not re-done here).
- Checked whether `offset_correction` (`route.py:203-206,218`,
  `math.log(2.0*math.exp(beta*absJ))/beta` per mediated edge) could be
  mis-accumulated across **multiple** mediated edges in a larger model --
  the triangle cases each mediate only one edge, so a second instance (K4,
  which mediates 2 edges) was built specifically to close this gap: the
  marginal-preservation property holds to `1.110e-16` with two mediators'
  worth of `offset_correction` accumulated. No finding.
