**Method:** the brief asks for four specific hunts plus a test-potency
exercise. Each is addressed below with live evidence (a real script run, or
a literal reading of the exact assertion in question), not with inference
from a docstring. Two genuinely new instances were found of the defect

**F2: `fmt_value`'s blanket `.6g` formatting is applied identically to
exact/derived floats (where it is honest) and to floats measured as
proportions over a finite sample (where it overstates precision by
roughly three orders of magnitude on this receipt's own numbers).**
`demo/lattice_app.py:1089-1100`, used at `:3009` (`fmt_value(val)` for
every VERIFICATION-panel float, including `task_validity` and
`codeword_violation_rate`) and elsewhere (`:2989-2990` for gate
`measured`/`limit` values, `:3170`ish-adjacent `j_max`, `beta`, etc.).
Verified live, this session (`PYTHONIOENCODING=utf-8 .../python.exe`,
against `demo/receipts/small/verification.json`, not hand-derived): with
`n=6400` (`_VERIFY_SAMPLE_PARAMS`'s own `n_chains=32 * n_samples=200`,
`src/tsu_compiler/passes/search.py:513-514,596-597`), `task_validity=0.24703125`
carries a binomial standard error of **0.0054** (`sqrt(p(1-p)/n)`) -- i.e.
the value is honestly known to about `0.247 +/- 0.005`, roughly **2**
significant figures -- yet `fmt_value` renders it as `"0.247031"`, **6**
significant figures, implying precision about **3 orders of magnitude**
finer than the sample supports. `codeword_violation_rate=0.01328125`
(SE=0.0014, true precision ~1-2 sig figs) renders as `"0.0132812"`, the
same category of overclaim. **This is receipt-specific in its current
manifestation** (on the audited "small" receipt, `energy_tv`/
`execution_tv`/`cross_check_tv` are all `None`/`"unavailable: ..."` because
192 nodes exceeds `EXACT_LIMIT`, so those three sampling-derived TV-distance
fields do not currently exhibit the same overclaim) **but is a structural
gap, not a one-off:** any exactly-enumerable receipt (e.g. the toy specs
`EXACT_LIMIT=18` is sized for) that populates `energy_tv`/`execution_tv`
would hit the identical issue, since `execution_tv = float(0.5 * np.abs(hist
- cond_probs).sum())` (`search.py:721`) is itself a statistic of an
empirical histogram, sampling-noisy in exactly the same way. **In contrast**,
`beta`, `onsager_betac`, `j_max` (`np.abs(...).max()` over exact program
weights) are deterministic/derived, not measured-over-a-sample, so 4-6 sig
figs is honest for those -- `fmt_value` does not distinguish the two
categories at all, applying one formatting policy to both. **Concrete
failure scenario:** a viewer (or a screenshot circulated outside this
session) reads "task_validity: 0.247031" and reasonably treats the sixth
digit as meaningful -- e.g. comparing two receipts' `task_validity` at the
`0.2470` vs `0.2471` level, when the actual measurement cannot distinguish
them (both are well inside one standard error of each other, and inside
even a fairly generous 2-SE band). **What was checked:** read `fmt_value`
in full and every call site of it across the VERIFICATION panel loop
(`:3004-3013`); computed the binomial SE for both live fields directly from
`verification.json`'s own recorded values and `_VERIFY_SAMPLE_PARAMS`'s own
`n_chains*n_samples`, reproduced above; confirmed `energy_tv`/
`execution_tv`/`cross_check_tv` are `None` for this specific receipt (so
this exact overclaim is latent, not currently rendered, for those three
fields) by reading `demo/receipts/small/verification.json` directly.
**Not performed (Phase F candidate):** either compute and display an
explicit uncertainty alongside any sampling-derived proportion (the
information is cheaply available: `n` is already known wherever these
fields are computed), or cap displayed precision for that category of
field at a number of significant figures derived from `n` rather than a
fixed `.6g` applied uniformly.

## Test-potency exercise: three assertions, checked against what they can and cannot observe

Per the brief: picked three assertions guarding behaviour this wave's
findings, R12's, and R4/C-1's all depend on, and read each one's exact body
against the production code path it is meant to guard -- not run-and-see
(a source mutation to verify this empirically was attempted and was
correctly refused by this session's own tooling safeguards, consistent
with the audit's own "no behaviour changes" rule; the analysis below is
therefore a direct, literal reading of each assertion's scope, which for a
pure substring/dict-shape check is as conclusive as running it).

**1. `test_speed_levels_never_names_warmup_or_steps`
(`tests/test_lattice_app_logic.py:302-307`) -- would NOT catch a
caller-side regression of the exact kind R12 rules out.** Its full body:
`for lvl in la.SPEED_LEVELS: assert "n_warmup" not in lvl; assert
"steps_per_sample" not in lvl`. This inspects only the **static
`SPEED_LEVELS` tuple's own dict keys** -- it never calls
`SampleWorker._run_unclamped_tick`/`_run_clamped_batch`, never constructs a
`SampleWorker`, never inspects a `sampler_params` dict. A regression that
left `SPEED_LEVELS` untouched but made `_run_unclamped_tick` derive its
`n_warmup` from `speed_level(self.speed_idx)["chains"]` via some new
in-function arithmetic (e.g. `N_WARMUP if n_chains >= BATCH_CHAINS else
N_WARMUP // 3`) -- exactly the category of bug R12's own Minor finding
describes -- would leave this test's every assertion true, because none of
them look at the caller at all. **This is a real gap, not a hypothetical
one: no OTHER test in the suite asserts the ACTUAL `sampler_params["n_warmup"]`
passed to `thrml_sample`/`simulate` stays constant across `speed_idx`
values either** (grepped `tests/test_lattice_app_logic.py` for `n_warmup`:
the only three hits are this test's own body and its section-header
comment).

**2. `test_batch_feasibility_all_invalid_is_infeasible`
(`tests/test_lattice_app_logic.py:265-270`) -- would NOT catch the exact
grammar regression R6's own finding already describes, independently
reconfirmed here by reading the literal assertion rather than R6's prose
summary of it.** Full body: `assert infeasible is True; assert "0 valid" in
reason; assert "3 draws" in reason`. Both content assertions are bare
substring-containment checks (`in`, not `==`, not a regex anchoring the
subject of the sentence). A rewrite of `batch_feasibility`'s reason string
from the current *"no valid world satisfies these pins -- {total} draws, 0
valid"* to a MORE absolute, MORE overclaiming *"these pins can never be
satisfied under any configuration -- {total} draws, 0 valid"* would leave
`"0 valid" in reason` and `"{n} draws" in reason` both still true (both
literal substrings are still present), so this test would pass unchanged
against a strictly worse regression on exactly the axis R6's own Important
finding is about (the main clause's grammatical subject/certainty).
**Independent confirmation of R6's claim, not a re-statement of it:** R6.md
asserted this from reading the test; this task re-derived the same
conclusion directly from the assertion's own literal semantics (`in` vs.
`==`) without reference to R6's prose, and they agree.

**3. `test_estimator_discriminates_iid_from_strongly_correlated`
(`tests/test_ess.py:178-196`) -- genuinely potent for what it tests, and
that is exactly why it is the interesting case: its potency does not
extend to covering C-1.** Its body draws `iid` (white noise) and `corr`
(phi=0.98 AR(1)) samples and asserts `r_iid.ess/r_iid.n_total > 50 *
(r_corr.ess/r_corr.n_total)` -- a real discriminative-power check that
WOULD fail immediately against a broken `effective_sample_size` (e.g. one
that returned a constant ESS regardless of input, or one whose windowing
logic silently degenerated to always reporting `ess ~= n_total`). This is
a well-constructed potency control exactly as its own docstring claims.
**But it, and every other test in `test_ess.py`, only ever calls
`integrated_autocorrelation_time`/`effective_sample_size` directly on
`ar1_chains(...)`-constructed, correctly-shaped `(n_chains, n_samples)`
arrays** -- grepped the full file: every one of its ~15 tests constructs
its own input via `ar1_chains`, never via anything resembling
`demo/lattice_app.py`'s actual `energy_trace.ys` (a flattened,
restart-and-chain-concatenated 1-D series). **No test anywhere in the
suite calls `render_acf_plot` at all** (grepped `tests/` for
`render_acf_plot`: the only two files matching are
`test_lattice_app_logic.py`, whose own comment at `:832` explicitly notes
`render_acf_plot` is exercised only "indirectly, via the app," never
directly, and `test_ess.py`, which imports `integrated_autocorrelation_time`
for its own unrelated direct tests, never through `lattice_app.py`'s call
site). **This is the structural reason C-1 exists and was never caught by
CI:** the estimator's own component tests are excellent and would catch a
regression IN THE ESTIMATOR; they provide zero coverage of the CONTRACT
BOUNDARY between the estimator and its one live caller, which is exactly
where the actual, already-filed defect lives. A reviewer trusting
`test_ess.py`'s evident rigor to imply "the tau/ACF feature is
well-tested" would be extending that trust past where the tests actually
reach.
