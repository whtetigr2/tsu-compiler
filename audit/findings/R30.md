# R30 -- two readouts, the same draws, opposite answers

**Status:** MEASURED. A result about which projection a sampling chip should
export, with a worked case.

## Why this was asked

A thermodynamic computer never hands you a distribution. It hands you draws, and
every quantity you compute from them is a lossy projection of something you
cannot observe directly. Which projection you can afford to export is therefore
an interface decision, not post-processing.

That is easy to say and worth checking. This checks it on a shipped program with
two projections that are already built.

## The setup

`prog_alloy_ordering_8x8`: an ordering alloy, unlike neighbours preferred, which
is a textbook antiferromagnet. Its ground state is a checkerboard, and there are
TWO of them, related by exchanging the species. That degeneracy is the whole
story below.

768 draws pooled from 12 independent chains at beta 0.9. Both phases appear,
305 draws in one and 458 in the other.

Ground truth, which needs the full configuration: the staggered magnetisation
per draw averages 0.755. The system is strongly ordered.

## The result

    projection            cost            says
    per-spin marginals    N numbers       mean |<s_i>| = 0.170, no order visible
    structure factor      N numbers       peak at (pi, pi), 156x background

Same draws. Same export cost. Opposite conclusions, and the marginals are wrong.

## Why

Marginals are not invariant under the model's own symmetry. Exchanging the two
species maps one ground state onto the other, so pooling draws from both phases
averages every site towards zero. The order is still there in every individual
draw; the projection destroys it.

S(k) is invariant under that symmetry, because |FFT|^2 discards the global sign.
The same insensitivity that makes it unable to locate a pattern is what lets it
survive the degeneracy.

**The symmetry that makes a ground state degenerate is exactly what breaks a
marginal readout.** Degeneracy is not exotic: it is the normal situation for an
antiferromagnet, a spin glass, and anything with a symmetry the Hamiltonian
respects.

## The corollary, which is the part worth acting on

Varying how many independent chains are pooled:

    chains pooled   phases seen   marginals say      S(k) says
                1             1   ORDER   0.830      order 203x
                2             2   nothing 0.112      order 163x
                4             2   ORDER   0.456      order 171x
               12             2   nothing 0.169      order 156x

Two things fall out.

The marginal readout is **unstable**. With four chains it said ORDER and with
twelve it said nothing, and the difference is only how the chains happened to
split between degenerate phases. S(k) reported 203, 163, 171 and 156 across the
same four conditions: tight, and correct in every one.

And the single chain, which never left one phase, produced the cleanest looking
marginals in the table. **A marginal readout looks best exactly when the sampler
is worst.** A chip that exports marginals and mixes poorly will show beautiful,
stable, confident numbers, and they will be an artefact of the chain being stuck.

That is a failure mode with no warning attached. Nothing in a marginal readout
says "these numbers are clean because I have not equilibrated".

## What this does and does not establish

It establishes that two projections of equal export cost can disagree about a
basic question on a real shipped workload, and that one of them fails in a way
correlated with sampler quality.

It does not establish a general theory of which projection to export. One model,
one temperature, one pair of projections. The exponential-family fact is the
nearest thing to a theory that already exists: a Boltzmann machine on graph G is
an exponential family whose minimal sufficient statistics are the site values
and the edge products, so for TRAINING that model anything else is redundant or
incomplete. That is a design rule and it is not implemented as an on-chip
accumulator anywhere this project has looked.

No energy claim. Everything ran on CPU through THRML.
