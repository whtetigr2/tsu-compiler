# R24 -- a preflight for someone else's silicon found the model was mistuned

**Status:** FOUND, and it is a gain rather than a retraction. The shipped
coherence constant was about half its best value, which cost ~30 points of
accuracy on a noisy input and nothing at all on a clean one -- so nothing
looked wrong.

**Method:** asking whether the game's energy survives the 4-bit coefficient
range of Hitachi's CMOS annealing ASIC. The quantisation grid turned out to
contain better-tuned points than the hand-chosen float constants, which is how
the mistuning surfaced.

## The quantisation question

Sampling depends on beta*J, not J, so coefficients can be scaled freely if the
temperature scales with them: quantise to round(s*J) and sample at beta/s. The
constraint is that the largest coefficient must still land in [-7, +7]. Here
that is the wall bias at 4.0, so s <= 1.75 -- a small budget.

Swept scales, five seeds each, 8% of free cells misreported as wall, since a
CLEAN input tests nothing about the couplings (R23):

     scale chain  coh free wall   b*Jc   b*Jh  noisy (mean)           range
      0.75     1    1    2   -3   2.93   2.93         63.3%   [60.5%-64.5%]
      1.00     2    1    3   -4   4.40   2.20         75.3%   [74.5%-76.0%]
      1.25     2    2    4   -5   3.52   3.52         87.2%   [86.5%-87.5%]
      1.50     2    2    4   -6   2.93   2.93         63.3%   [60.5%-64.5%]
      1.75     3    2    5   -7   3.77   2.51         74.4%   [73.0%-75.5%]

Float reference: 68.1% [65.5%-70.5%].

**The model survives quantisation comfortably.** Scale 1.25 is legal, in range,
and scores 87.2%.

An internal check worth keeping: scales 0.75 and 1.50 produce different
integers but identical effective couplings (2.93, 2.93) and identical scores
(63.3%). Only beta*J matters, and the sweep reproduces that exactly.

## The part that was not the question

Several quantised points BEAT the float model, which quantisation cannot do.
The effective-coupling columns say why: scale 1.25 runs coherence at 3.52 where
the float model runs 2.64. It is not rounding better, it is tuned better.

Retuning the float model directly, three seeds a cell:

    W_MONO  W_COH    clean  2% noise  8% noise  14% noise
       3.0    1.2   100.0%     97.2%     68.2%      26.0%   <- shipped
       3.2    1.6   100.0%     98.7%     87.0%      53.7%
       3.2    2.0   100.0%     99.5%     92.3%      66.8%
       3.2    2.4   100.0%     99.5%     96.3%      72.8%   <- best
       3.2    3.0   100.0%     99.5%     96.2%      66.8%
       3.2    4.0   100.0%     99.5%     93.7%      57.8%

It turns over after 2.4, so this is an optimum and not a slope. The clean column
never moves, which is exactly why this went unnoticed: at zero noise the biases
alone give the right answer and the coherence term is invisible.

Shipped to the game. Its own ablate() now reports, at 8% noise, 3.2% with the
couplings off against 99.2% with them on -- previously 3.9% against 83.5%. At
10% noise the rendered view is visually indistinguishable from the clean one.

## Why this was nearly missed, again

The first version of this check ran only on a clean input and reported that
every scale held 100%. That is a true measurement of nothing: R23 established
that the couplings are inert on clean occupancy, so a clean-input quantisation
test cannot say whether quantisation damaged them. The test had to move to the
regime where the term being tested does something.

Same shape as R23. A control that cannot fail is not a control.

## What it does not show

Nothing here has touched silicon. This is a precondition check. Whether that
machine samples at a fixed temperature or only anneals toward low-energy states
is unresolved, and it is the question that decides whether a port would produce
samples or merely optima.
