# R28 -- protein folding works, and does not fit Z1

**Status:** MEASURED. Not a retraction. A capability boundary, found by taking a
real problem end to end.

`audit/protein_folding.py` runs the whole thing and prints every number below.

## The problem

HP lattice protein folding. A chain of hydrophobic and polar monomers folds on a
square lattice, and the fold that buries the most hydrophobic contacts wins. Lau
and Dill introduced it in 1989. The literature's own name for it is "the Ising
model of protein folding" (arXiv:1102.0308), and it is the benchmark D-Wave used
to demonstrate quantum annealing on hardware (Perdomo-Ortiz et al., *Sci. Rep.*
2, 2012).

The instance: **HHPPHPPH**, eight monomers, on a 3x3 grid.

## Ground truth, computed first and never adjusted

By exhaustive enumeration of every self-avoiding walk, written in the same file
and depending on nothing else in this repository:

    2,172 walks
    optimal contacts 3
    reached by 8 walks, which is 0.37% of the space

That number is a property of the sequence and the lattice. It does not depend on
the encoding, the compiler, or the sampler, which is the only reason it is worth
checking against.

## Three results, and they have different answers

### The model is right

Sampled through THRML over a 15-colour block schedule, 24 chains, 4,000 warmup
sweeps:

    valid folds        9,525 of 9,600 draws   (99.22%)
    best contacts      3
    optimum            3
    best fold          (0,2) (1,2) (2,2) (2,1) (1,1) (1,0) (0,0) (0,1)

It reaches a fold that 0.37% of the space achieves, in about two seconds of
sampling. "Valid" is checked on the decoded chain, not inferred from the energy:
one site per monomer, no two monomers sharing a site, and every consecutive pair
lattice-adjacent.

### The bias cap is a units problem, not a limit

The natural encoding produces |b| up to 70.0 against a cap of 6.0. That reads as
a hardware refusal and is not one. Scaling every coefficient by s and beta by 1/s
leaves the distribution identical, so the cap is satisfied by choosing units:

    scale 0.0857, beta 11.7   ->   |J| 0.343, |b| 6.000, gate passes

Worth separating explicitly, because the next failure is not like this.

### Z1 cannot host it, and the reason is structural

    max_degree    35 against a cap of 16    FAIL

Scaling cannot touch this. The degree comes from two cliques that **add**:

    one-hot over sites    (m - 1) = 8 neighbours
    exclusion over monomers (n - 1) = 7 neighbours
    ------------------------------------------------
    floor before any chain or contact term      15

With a cap of 16, that leaves one neighbour of headroom for the chain
connectivity and the contact reward, which need at least four. And a fold always
needs at least as many sites as monomers, so m >= n and the floor is at least
2n - 2 for any instance.

Measured across lengths, taking the smallest grid that can hold each chain:

    len   grid   vars   max degree   clique floor   fits 16
      4   2x3      24       16             8          no
      5   2x3      30       17             9          no
      6   3x3      54       29            13          no
      7   3x3      63       34            14          no
      8   3x3      72       35            15          no
      9   3x4     108       49            19          no

**The occupancy encoding of HP folding does not fit Z1 at any useful length**,
including a four-monomer chain. This is not about instance size. It is about two
cliques whose degrees add faster than the cap grows.

## What this is not

It is not a claim about protein structure prediction. A lattice toy model is to a
folding prediction what a wind tunnel model is to an airplane: real, useful, and
not the airplane.

It is not an energy claim. Everything here ran on CPU through THRML.

It is not a statement that the problem is unreachable. It is a statement about
one encoding, which is precisely the encoding the quantum annealing literature
also found expensive, and which is why Babbush, Perdomo-Ortiz and others went
looking for alternatives.

## What would change the answer

A sparser encoding. The compiler's own remediation says so, and the literature
agrees: turn-based and "diamond" encodings exist specifically to cut the resource
cost of lattice folding on constrained hardware. Neither is implemented here, and
neither has been measured against Z1.

The one attempted here, replacing the dense non-adjacency penalty with a sparse
adjacency reward, does not help: it changes which edges exist rather than how
many, and the clique floor is untouched.
