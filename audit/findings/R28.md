# R28 -- protein folding fits Z1, once you delete what cannot happen

**Status:** MEASURED, resolved. The first version of this finding concluded that
HP folding does not fit Z1 at any useful length. That conclusion was wrong, and
the reason it was wrong is the interesting part.

`audit/protein_folding.py` runs the whole thing and prints every number below.

## The problem

HP lattice protein folding. A chain of hydrophobic and polar monomers folds on a
square lattice, and the fold burying the most hydrophobic contacts wins. Lau and
Dill, 1989. The literature's own name for the model is "the Ising model of
protein folding" (arXiv:1102.0308), and it is the benchmark D-Wave used to
demonstrate quantum annealing on hardware (Perdomo-Ortiz et al., *Sci. Rep.* 2,
2012).

The instance: **HHPPHPPH**, eight monomers, on a 3x3 grid.

## Ground truth, computed first and never adjusted

Exhaustive enumeration of every self-avoiding walk, written in the same file and
depending on nothing else in this repository:

    2,172 walks
    optimal contacts 3
    reached by 8 walks, which is 0.37% of the space

That number belongs to the sequence and the lattice. It does not move when the
encoding changes, which is the only reason it is worth checking against.

## The refusal, and what it was really saying

The natural occupancy encoding gives one binary variable per (monomer, site)
pair. Against Z1:

    max_abs_coupling    ok      |J| 4.0 against a cap of 6.0
    max_abs_bias        FAIL    |b| 70.0 against a cap of 6.0
    max_degree          FAIL    35 against a cap of 16

**The bias failure is a units problem, not a hardware limit.** Scaling every
coefficient by s and beta by 1/s leaves the distribution identical, so the cap is
met by choosing units. Worth separating, because the degree failure is not like
this and scaling cannot touch it.

The degree comes from two cliques that add:

    one-hot over sites        (m - 1) = 8 neighbours
    exclusion over monomers   (n - 1) = 7 neighbours
    -----------------------------------------------
    floor before any chain or contact term      15

A fold needs at least as many sites as monomers, so that floor is at least
2n - 2 for any instance. Measured, a four-monomer chain already exceeded the cap.

## Why that conclusion was premature

The comparison that prompted a second look: the game's visibility lattice is
**11,200 spins at max degree 4**, and it is also "objects in a shared space". So
shared space is not the problem.

The real distinction is what the variable is indexed by.

    the game     one variable per CELL              degree 4, independent of size
    folding      one variable per (MONOMER, SITE)   degree grows with both

A field couples each location to its neighbours. An assignment couples every
object to every other object at every location. That is the difference, and it
points at the fix: **delete the (monomer, site) pairs that cannot occur.**

## Parity pruning

A square lattice is bipartite, so a walk alternates sublattices at every step.
After i steps from a site of even parity the monomer is necessarily on a site
whose (x + y) parity matches i. **Half of all (monomer, site) pairs describe
configurations no chain can reach.**

Deleting them is exact, and a test enforces that:

    self-avoiding walks violating the parity rule: 0 of 2,172

Nothing real is lost. What goes is half the variables, half of both cliques, and
the degree failure.

    encoding                      vars   max degree   verdict   placed
    as built                        72        35        fail     False
    parity pruned                   36        15        warn     TRUE

Placed on Z1 with 74 mediators, on the first attempt.

## And it still solves the problem

Sampled through THRML over a 6-colour block schedule, 24 chains, 4,000 warmup
sweeps:

    valid folds     9,503 of 9,600 draws   (98.99%)
    best contacts   3
    optimum         3
    best fold       (2,2) (1,2) (0,2) (0,1) (1,1) (1,0) (2,0) (2,1)

About one second of sampling to reach a fold that 0.37% of the space achieves.
"Valid" is checked on the decoded chain, never inferred from a low energy: one
site per monomer, no two monomers sharing a site, every consecutive pair
adjacent.

## What this is not

Not a claim about protein structure prediction. A lattice toy model is to a
folding prediction what a wind tunnel model is to an airplane: real, useful, and
not the airplane.

Not an energy claim. Everything here ran on CPU through THRML.

Not a claim that every instance fits. One sequence, eight monomers, one grid.
The parity argument is general, but the degree floor still grows with chain
length, and where the pruned encoding stops fitting has not been measured.

## Why photonic Ising machines did not hit this

Coherent and spatial photonic Ising machines are typically all-to-all connected,
so degree is not a constraint they have. Z1 is sparse by design: sixteen
neighbours at sixteen fixed relative positions. Dense hardware can take the naive
encoding; sparse hardware needs the encoding to be sparse too.

That is not a deficiency in either. It is the trade that buys Z1 its density, and
it is exactly the trade a compiler exists to make visible.
