  The diagonals carry 7x LESS correlation than the edges you
  would pick by looking at the data, and couplings of the same order.
  They are also indispensable: dropping them is the `nearest neighbour`
  row above, where KL goes from 0.000 to 5.295. A heuristic that spends
  connectivity where the correlation is drops every one of them, and
  lands on `rook`, which is worse than a random graph of the same size.

  A capped fit does not match the moments and is not supposed to:
  it is the best a Z1-legal model can do, which is the question the
  compiler's |J| gate is asking. The moment error column says how far
  the cap pushes it off the target's own statistics.

KL(p||q)     nats from the target. 0 means the moment readout
             lost nothing: the model it determines IS the target.
valid        mass the fitted model puts on bars-and-stripes.
structured   that mass minus the two trivial uniform patterns,
             which any ferromagnet produces for free.

For reference, KL(target || uniform over all states) = 7.689 nats.
A KL printed as -0.000 is float noise below the precision of the
sum; the leaked-mass line above gives the honest magnitude.
